1. Bands, units and conventions

Everything is worked out in the eight octave bands 63 Hz to 8 kHz, one band at a time, with that band's absorption, scattering, air absorption and source pattern. The engine traces energy, not pressure: an arrival has an energy and a time but no phase, so interference between paths is not modelled (the line array is the one exception, see 4.4). Times are counted in bins of \Delta t = 1 ms. The source emits one unit of energy in each band, so every result is a ratio of energies, except where a sound power level is given.

Levels are 10 \lg of an energy ratio, where \lg = \log_{10}. Coordinates are in metres with z up. A unit vector is written \hat{u}, and \xi is a uniform random number in [0, 1).

2. The room's geometry

A room is a closed surface of flat polygon faces, plus any free-standing screens inside it. Each face is triangulated by ear clipping, with holes bridged to the outline first [Eberly 2002]. Rays find the nearest face through a bounding-volume hierarchy over the triangles, and each triangle is tested by the Möller-Trumbore method [Möller and Trumbore 1997]. A point is inside the room when a ray from it crosses the surface an odd number of times.

Volume and area

The volume comes from the divergence theorem, over the faces i with area A_i, outward normal \hat{n}_i and a corner \mathbf{x}_i, about any point \mathbf{c} (the centroid):

V = \frac{1}{3} \sum_i A_i \, \hat{n}_i \cdot (\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{c})

If the sum comes out negative, the faces were wound inward and are turned round. S is the total area of the closed surface.

Characteristic size of a surface

Faces that lie in one plane, share edges and have one finish make one surface. Its size, used for the edge scattering in 7.3, is the side of a square or the diameter of a disc of the same proportions:

a = \frac{4 A}{P}

where A is the surface's area and P its perimeter.

Checking and repairing an imported model

A model must be a closed surface. The geometry check lists each run of edges used by only one face (a hole), each edge used by more than two faces, and faces wound against their neighbours. The repair runs these steps in order:

  • Holes are filled. Each loop of open edges gets a plane fitted from the normals at its corners, and is triangulated in that plane, or in flat parts if it is not flat.
  • Corners closer than 2 mm on open or crowded edges are joined.
  • T-junctions, where a corner lies on another face's edge, are cut.
  • Parts that are not the room, such as furniture and hanging sheets, become screens, or optionally their bounding boxes.
  • Holes are filled again.

These steps are geometry housekeeping; they add no acoustics.

Editing the shape

A model's shape can also be changed by hand, in the 3-D view. Corners and faces are found by where they are, to within 1 mm, so a saved project finds them again in the model read afresh.

  • A corner moved takes every face that meets there with it. Corners that come together are joined, and faces left with no area are dropped.
  • A face drawn has its corners in order round it, and may be concave. They must lie in one plane, to within 2 cm or 1 % of the face's size, and the outline must not cross itself. The face is triangulated by ear clipping, and a corner within 1 mm of one of the model's is that corner, so the face joins its neighbours.
  • A surface deleted is the face clicked and the faces of its group joined to it edge to edge in its plane, as in a split.

3. Sound in air

Speed of sound

c = 331.3 + 0.606 \, \vartheta \quad \text{m/s}

where \vartheta is the air temperature in °C.

Air absorption (ISO 9613-1)

With absolute temperature T = \vartheta + 273.16 K, reference temperature T_0 = 293.15 K, pressure p_a, reference pressure p_r = 101325 Pa and relative humidity h_r (%):

\frac{p_\mathrm{sat}}{p_r} = 10^{-6.8346 \left( 273.16 / T \right)^{1.261} + 4.6151}
h = h_r \, \frac{p_\mathrm{sat} / p_r}{p_a / p_r}
f_{rO} = \frac{p_a}{p_r} \left( 24 + 4.04 \times 10^{4} \, h \, \frac{0.02 + h}{0.391 + h} \right)
f_{rN} = \frac{p_a}{p_r} \left( \frac{T}{T_0} \right)^{-1/2} \left( 9 + 280 \, h \, \exp \left\{ -4.17 \left[ \left( \frac{T}{T_0} \right)^{-1/3} - 1 \right] \right\} \right)
a = 8.686 \, f^2 \left[ 1.84 \times 10^{-11} \left( \frac{p_r}{p_a} \right) \left( \frac{T}{T_0} \right)^{1/2} + \left( \frac{T}{T_0} \right)^{-5/2} \left( \frac{0.01275 \, e^{-2239.1/T}}{f_{rO} + f^2/f_{rO}} + \frac{0.1068 \, e^{-3352/T}}{f_{rN} + f^2/f_{rN}} \right) \right] \quad \text{dB/m}

Here f is the band's centre frequency (the exact centre for third octaves). Along a path of length \ell the energy is multiplied by 10^{-a \ell / 10}. The 273.16 (not 273.15) is kept from the VBA original; it changes nothing at the precision of the standard's tables. For the reverberation formulas, the intensity attenuation coefficient is m = a / (10 \lg e) Np/m, and the air's absorption area is 4 m V.

4. Sources

Directivity

A source radiates with a relative energy D(\hat{u}) in each direction, normalised so that its mean over the sphere is 1. That keeps the radiated power the same as an omnidirectional source's (D = 1):

D(\hat{u}) = \frac{P(\hat{u})}{\langle P \rangle}, \quad \langle P \rangle = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} P(\hat{u}_i)

where P is the pattern as given, 10^{L/10} of its level in dB. The mean is taken over a Fibonacci lattice of N = 20000 points [González 2010], z_i = 1 - 2(i + 1/2)/N, \varphi_i = i \, \pi (3 - \sqrt{5}), which is nearly uniform and does not depend on a random seed. Each band has its own pattern, normalised on its own.

The patterns are:

  • Horn, from the −6 dB coverage angles \Theta_H and \Theta_V. At azimuth \phi off axis and elevation \theta, on a 2.5° grid:
    L(\phi, \theta) = \max \left\{ L_\mathrm{floor}, \; -6 \left[ \left( \frac{\phi \cos\theta}{\Theta_H / 2} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\theta}{\Theta_V / 2} \right)^2 \right] \right\} \quad \text{dB}
  • Balloon (a CSV upload, a CLF text or binary .CF2 file, an EASE 3 or 4 balloon (.lob), or a CIF pattern from BYU's directivity archive): a level on a grid of azimuth and elevation, interpolated bilinearly in dB. It wraps round in azimuth and is held at the end rows in elevation.
  • Polar: a level against the angle from the axis, interpolated linearly in dB.
  • Talker: Chu and Warnock's balloon, the mean of 40 talkers [Chu and Warnock 2002]. Its third octaves are turned into octaves by adding energies, and the 63 Hz and 125 Hz octaves take the 160 Hz third.

A pattern is aimed by an azimuth and an elevation, with the world's up kept as up (no roll).

Several elements: lines, surfaces, clusters

An extended source is a set of point elements that radiate incoherently, so their energies add. Element k has a share w_{k,b} of the power in band b, with \sum_k w_{k,b} = 1, and is traced with that share of the rays. Its response is added with weight w_{k,b}, after a delay \tau_k if it has one. A delay that is not a whole number of bins is split linearly between the two bins either side, so no energy is lost. A surface source is a grid of elements about 0.5 m apart, at most 8 each way, on a rectangle facing where the source is aimed.

Coherent line array

For N elements at spacing d, element i sits s_i = i - (N - 1)/2 spacings from the middle, at \mathbf{p}_i. It has amplitude weight w_i, its own pattern a_i(\hat{u}) (turned by its splay) and a delay \tau_i = i \, \Delta\tau. In the far field, in the direction \hat{u}:

p(\hat{u}, \omega) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} w_i \, a_i(\hat{u}) \, e^{\, j \left( k \, \hat{u} \cdot \mathbf{p}_i - \omega \tau_i \right)}, \quad k = \frac{\omega}{c}

Its level is \lvert p \rvert^2. The delay step steers the beam by \sin\theta_s = c \, \Delta\tau / d. One frequency has nulls far deeper than a band's, so each octave band is the energy mean of seven frequencies a sixth of an octave apart round its centre, held to no more than 60 dB below its peak. The weights (shading) are:

w_i^\mathrm{Hamming} = 0.54 - 0.46 \cos\frac{2\pi i}{N-1}, \quad w_i^\mathrm{Hann} = \frac{1}{2} \left( 1 - \cos\frac{2\pi (i+1)}{N+1} \right), \quad w_i^\mathrm{sine} = \sin\frac{\pi (i + 1/2)}{N}

Dolph-Chebyshev weights put every side lobe at a chosen level R (in dB) below the main beam [Dolph 1946]. The Chebyshev polynomial T_{N-1}(\beta \cos(\pi k / N)), with \beta = \cosh\left( \frac{1}{N-1} \operatorname{arcosh} 10^{R/20} \right), is sampled round the unit circle and transformed back to the element weights. This is the far field. It is fair beyond about L^2 / (2\lambda) from an array of length L.

5. Direct sound and image sources

The receiver

A receiver is a sphere of radius r (0.5 m unless set). Of the energy an isotropic source sends out, a sphere at distance d intercepts the fraction given by its solid angle over 4\pi:

F(d) = \frac{1}{2} \left( 1 - \sqrt{1 - \frac{r^2}{d^2}} \right) \approx \frac{r^2}{4 d^2}

This is the expected share of rays that enter the sphere, so the direct sound and image sources (worked out exactly) are on the same scale as the ray-traced energy (counted by hits).

Direct sound

E_\mathrm{dir} = F(d) \, D(\hat{u}_{SR}) \, 10^{-a d / 10} \, \Gamma_\mathrm{screens}, \quad t = \frac{d}{c}

where d = \lvert \mathbf{R} - \mathbf{S} \rvert. There is no direct sound if a wall is in the way (it may still bend round a corner of the room, section 8), and \Gamma_\mathrm{screens} is what passes any screens (section 8). The arrival goes in bin \lfloor t / \Delta t \rfloor.

Image sources

The source \mathbf{S} is mirrored in the plane of each face, with inward normal \hat{n} and a point \mathbf{x}_0 on the face, and the image is mirrored again, up to the chosen order (at most 10) [Allen and Berkley 1979; Borish 1984]:

\mathbf{S}' = \mathbf{S} - 2 \, \hat{n} \left[ \hat{n} \cdot (\mathbf{S} - \mathbf{x}_0) \right]

A face is tried only if the image is in front of it. A sequence of faces is a real path if the line from the receiver back to each image in turn crosses that image's face inside its polygon. In a room that is not convex, or that has screens, each leg must also be clear of the walls and must reach each face from inside the room. A screen reflects from both sides, but a path off it must arrive and leave on the same side. Paths that give the same image point (through an edge) are counted once. A path with reflections off faces k = 1 \ldots n and total length d' carries:

E_\mathrm{img} = \left[ \prod_{k=1}^{n} (1 - \alpha_k)(1 - s'_k) \right] D(\hat{u}_0) \, F(d') \, 10^{-a d' / 10} \, \prod_\mathrm{legs} \Gamma_\mathrm{screens}, \quad t = \frac{d'}{c}

Here \alpha_k is what the face takes (section 7) and s'_k its scattering, including the edge diffraction, worked out with the path's own legs. \hat{u}_0 is the direction the path leaves the source. The specular share (1 - s') is the image source's. The scattered share is left to the rays.

6. Ray tracing

In each band, N rays leave the source in uniformly random directions [Krokstad, Strøm and Sørsdal 1968; Vorländer 1989]:

z = 2\xi_1 - 1, \quad \varphi = 2\pi \xi_2, \quad \hat{u} = \left( \sqrt{1 - z^2} \cos\varphi, \; \sqrt{1 - z^2} \sin\varphi, \; z \right)

Each ray carries E_0 = D(\hat{u}) / N. Launching uniformly and weighting by the pattern is unbiased and needs no rejection step. Along a segment of length \ell, the energy is multiplied by 10^{-a \ell / 10}. At a face, it keeps (1 - \alpha) and leaves in one of these directions:

\hat{u}_\mathrm{spec} = \hat{u} - 2 (\hat{u} \cdot \hat{n}) \hat{n}
\hat{u}_\mathrm{Lambert} = \sin\theta \cos\varphi \, \hat{t}_1 + \sin\theta \sin\varphi \, \hat{t}_2 + \cos\theta \, \hat{n}, \quad \cos\theta = \sqrt{\xi_1}, \; \varphi = 2\pi\xi_2

Here \hat{n} is the face's normal on the side the ray arrives from, and \hat{t}_1, \hat{t}_2 complete an orthonormal frame. The Lambert direction is cosine-weighted (Lambert's law).

Scattering models

  • Probabilistic (the default): with probability s' the ray leaves in the Lambert direction, otherwise specularly. A purely specular path is then a real thing, which the image sources count exactly.
  • Vector-based [Christensen and Rindel 2005]: every ray leaves along
    \hat{u}_\mathrm{out} = \frac{(1 - s') \, \hat{u}_\mathrm{spec} + s' \, \hat{u}_\mathrm{Lambert}}{\left\lVert (1 - s') \, \hat{u}_\mathrm{spec} + s' \, \hat{u}_\mathrm{Lambert} \right\rVert}
    No ray stays exactly specular, so this model runs as pure ray tracing, whatever the image-source order.

Hybrid counting

The first segment of a ray is the direct sound, which is added analytically, so it is never counted. A later segment is counted if the ray has been scattered at least once, or has had more reflections than the image-source order. A purely specular path of low order is already counted once by the image sources. A counted segment from \mathbf{p} along \hat{u} enters receiver \mathbf{R} at:

\ell_e = (\mathbf{R} - \mathbf{p}) \cdot \hat{u} - \sqrt{r^2 - \left( \lvert \mathbf{R} - \mathbf{p} \rvert^2 - \left[ (\mathbf{R} - \mathbf{p}) \cdot \hat{u} \right]^2 \right)}

If 0 < \ell_e \le \ell, the ray deposits E \, 10^{-a \ell_e / 10} in the bin of time (L + \ell_e)/c, where L is the path so far. A ray ends when its energy falls 70 dB below its launch energy, or when it passes the length of the response. Each ray draws from its own SplitMix64 stream [Steele, Lea and Flood 2014], seeded by the run's seed and the ray's number. The answer is therefore the same on any number of processor cores, and the billiard-ball view draws the rays the calculation used.

With Lambert scattering at every wall, the decay sits between Eyring's and Sabine's, about a third of the way from Eyring's. Eyring assumes every ray reflects at the mean free path 4V/S [Kuttruff 2016]; the spread of reflection counts between rays lengthens the decay. A purely specular rectangular room does not mix its rays, and its decay is not exponential. Real rooms have the roughness that a scattering coefficient stands for.

7. Surfaces: absorption, scattering, transmission

7.1 Absorption

Each finish has an absorption coefficient \alpha_b in each band. A band with no data takes the nearest band that has it (ties go to the lower band). Library values above 1 are capped at 1.0, except per-person and per-fitting entries, which are absorption areas. An open surface (sound passes out) has \alpha = 1 and s = 0.

Audience areas

The edge effect of seating is allowed for by a 0.5 m strip along the exposed edge [Beranek 2004]. A seated area S_A with exposed edge length P_A gets:

\alpha' = \min \left\{ 1, \; \alpha \, \frac{S_A + 0.5 \, P_A}{S_A} \right\}

For an imported model, P_A is found from the group's outline: the edges its triangles do not share with each other. An outline edge counts as exposed unless a face beside it rises more than 0.3 m above it, as a wall or a high riser does. An edge beside an aisle, the stage or a step down is exposed, and so is one with nothing beside it. You can type over the value it finds.

Absorption by angle of incidence (optional)

With this setting, each reflection takes the absorption of the angle it meets the surface at. The surface is treated as locally reacting, with a real normalised impedance \xi [Kuttruff 2016]:

R(\theta) = \frac{\xi \cos\theta - 1}{\xi \cos\theta + 1}, \qquad \alpha(\theta) = 1 - R^2 = \frac{4 \xi \cos\theta}{(\xi \cos\theta + 1)^2}
\alpha_\mathrm{random} = 2 \int_0^1 \alpha(\theta) \cos\theta \; d(\cos\theta) = \frac{8}{\xi^2} \left[ 1 + \xi - \frac{1}{1 + \xi} - 2 \ln(1 + \xi) \right]

Each surface's \xi is chosen so that this diffuse-field (Paris) average equals its coefficient, on the high-impedance side. The average is largest, 0.951, at \xi \approx 1.57; a coefficient above that takes this \xi and has its reflection 1 - \alpha(\theta) scaled to match. Effects:

  • In a diffuse field: each surface absorbs what its coefficient says; only the share between angles changes.
  • Head-on sound is absorbed less than the coefficient.
  • Oblique sound is absorbed more, up to all of it where \xi\cos\theta = 1, close to grazing for a hard surface.
  • Sound right at grazing is hardly absorbed at all.

The image sources use the angle of each path's arriving leg, and the rays use their own. Seating is not a plain locally reacting surface, and the low-frequency seat dip is not modelled.

7.2 Scattering

The scattering coefficient s (as defined in ISO 17497-1) is the share of the reflected energy that is not specular.

7.3 Scattering by the edges' diffraction

A finite surface cannot reflect specularly below a limit frequency, and what it does not reflect is spread by diffraction at its edges [Rindel 1986; Christensen and Rindel 2005]. Take a surface of size a (from section 2), met at angle \theta to its normal, by sound that has travelled d_1 to it and goes on d_2:

a^{*} = \frac{2 d_1 d_2}{d_1 + d_2}, \quad f_g = \frac{c \, a^{*}}{2 \left( a \cos\theta \right)^2}
s_d = \begin{cases} 1 - f / f_g, \; f < f_g \\ 0, \; f \ge f_g \end{cases} \qquad s' = 1 - (1 - s)(1 - s_d)

For an image path, d_1 and d_2 are its legs. For a ray, d_1 is the segment that arrived, and d_2 is how far the specular reflection would go to the next face. That is looked up only when it could matter: f_g is largest, at c \, d_1 / (a\cos\theta)^2, when d_2 is endless.

7.4 Transmission through screens and partitions

A screen or partition with sound reduction R in a band lets through \tau = 10^{-R/10}, and reflects 1 - \max(\alpha, \tau). The ray goes on through the face with probability \tau / \kappa, otherwise it reflects, and in either case it carries \kappa:

\kappa = 1 - \alpha + \tau

On average, then, \tau goes through and 1 - \alpha comes back. A ray that passes through is not counted as a reflection. The boundary walls have nothing beyond them, so their R is not used.

8. Screens and diffraction

Sound reaches a point behind a free-standing screen by bending round its free edges: the top, and any side not against a wall. For an edge from \mathbf{A} to \mathbf{B}, the shortest route from \mathbf{S} to \mathbf{R} by way of the edge meets it at \mathbf{P}. Unfolded about the edge, the route is straight, so \mathbf{P} divides the feet of \mathbf{S} and \mathbf{R} on the edge in the ratio of their distances from it. The path difference and Fresnel number are:

\delta = \lvert \mathbf{SP} \rvert + \lvert \mathbf{PR} \rvert - \lvert \mathbf{SR} \rvert, \quad N = \frac{2\delta}{\lambda}

Each edge is cut by the Kurze-Anderson closed form of Maekawa's thin-barrier curve [Maekawa 1968; Kurze and Anderson 1971]:

\Delta L(N) = 5 + 20 \lg \frac{\sqrt{2\pi N}}{\tanh\sqrt{2\pi N}} \quad \text{dB}

The edges' energies add, held to within 20 dB of the unscreened sound, the usual limit for one thin barrier (as in ISO 9613-2). A partition adds what passes through the face the line crosses. Screens in a line multiply:

\Gamma_\mathrm{screens} = \prod_\mathrm{screens} \min \left\{ 1, \; \mathrm{clamp} \left( \sum_\mathrm{edges} 10^{-\Delta L / 10}, \; 10^{-2}, \; 1 \right) + \tau \right\}

A panel with no free edge lets through only its \tau. The edge paths themselves are not checked for walls or other screens. The factor is applied to the direct sound and to each image-source leg a screen stands across.

Round the room's own corners

Where the walls hide the source from a receiver, the direct sound bends round the room's outward-pointing edges. These are the edges where two faces of the closed surface fold away from the room: the corner of an L-shaped room, the front of a balcony, a pillar. A face folds away when its far corner lies behind the other face's plane. Each such edge both points can see gives a path over the shortest route S \to P \to R round it, found as for a screen's edge. That path's energy is:

E = F(\lvert SP \rvert + \lvert PR \rvert) \, D(\hat{u}_{SP}) \, 10^{-a (\lvert SP \rvert + \lvert PR \rvert)/10} \, 10^{-\min\{\Delta L(N), \, 20\}/10}, \quad N = \frac{2\delta}{\lambda}

It arrives at (\lvert SP \rvert + \lvert PR \rvert)/c, from P, and the paths' energies add.

With image sources, a reflection a corner hides bends round it too, with one or two reflections all before the bend or all after it (images of the source, or of the receiver). For an image \mathbf{S}' of the source, the route \mathbf{S}' \to \mathbf{P} \to \mathbf{R} is unfolded about the edge as above. It counts only if:

  • the corner shadows the receiver from the image: the real part of the line \mathbf{S}'\mathbf{R}, from where it leaves the last face it reflects from, passes through one of the two faces that meet at the edge (out of the shadow, the reflection's own path is the sound there);
  • the reflection's own specular path is not valid;
  • the reflections from \mathbf{S} to \mathbf{P} are a valid image path, and \mathbf{PR} is clear of the walls.

The bend first and reflections after use an image \mathbf{R}' of the receiver in the same way. Such a path has the reflections' weight \prod (1-\alpha)(1-s) of the image paths, times the bend's factor 10^{-\min\{\Delta L(N), \, 20\}/10} with \delta = \lvert \mathbf{S}'\mathbf{P} \rvert + \lvert \mathbf{PR} \rvert - \lvert \mathbf{S}'\mathbf{R} \rvert, spread and absorbed by the air over its whole length.

A route round two corners is not found, nor one with reflections on both sides of the bend. A wall drawn as several faces shadows only through the faces that meet at the edge. A wedge diffracts less than a thin screen deep into its shadow, so this overstates the sound there, as the 20 dB limit does.

9. The response and its decay

The result at a receiver is the energy response h_i: the energy in bin i, time t_i = i\,\Delta t, in each band. Following ISO 3382-1, all times are counted from the arrival of the direct sound, t_0, the first bin with energy in the band. In what follows, E(t_1, t_2) is the energy in the bins that start at or after t_0 + t_1 and before t_0 + t_2.

The decay curve is Schroeder's backward integral [Schroeder 1965]:

L(t) = 10 \lg \frac{\int_t^{\infty} h(\tau) \, d\tau}{\int_0^{\infty} h(\tau) \, d\tau}

A least-squares line L = L_0 + \beta t is fitted to the points between two levels. The decay time is the time for a 60 dB fall, T = -60 / \beta. The fit ranges are:

ParameterFit range
EDT0 to −10 dB
T20−5 to −25 dB
T30−5 to −35 dB

A fit needs at least two points and a falling line. The response must be long enough for the 35 dB range; the default is 2 s.

Lateral measures

Each arrival's energy is also weighted by the angle \psi between its direction and the receiver's lateral axis. That axis is horizontal and at right angles to the line to the source, as a figure-of-eight microphone is placed [Barron and Marshall 1981; Kleiner 1989; Bradley and Soulodre 1995]:

\hat{\ell} = \frac{(-f_y, \; f_x, \; 0)}{\sqrt{f_x^2 + f_y^2}}, \quad \mathbf{f} = \mathbf{S} - \mathbf{R}
LF_{80} = \frac{E_{\cos^2\psi}(5\,\mathrm{ms}, 80\,\mathrm{ms})}{E(0, 80\,\mathrm{ms})}, \quad LFC_{80} = \frac{E_{\lvert\cos\psi\rvert}(5\,\mathrm{ms}, 80\,\mathrm{ms})}{E(0, 80\,\mathrm{ms})}, \quad L_J = 10 \lg \frac{E_{\cos^2\psi}(80\,\mathrm{ms}, \infty)}{E_{10}}

11. Stage, echo and single numbers

Gade's stage measures

For a receiver about 1 m from the source, the first 10 ms holds the direct sound and the floor reflection [Gade 1989]:

ST_\mathrm{early} = 10 \lg \frac{E(20, 100\,\mathrm{ms})}{E(0, 10\,\mathrm{ms})}, \quad ST_\mathrm{late} = 10 \lg \frac{E(100\,\mathrm{ms}, 1\,\mathrm{s})}{E(0, 10\,\mathrm{ms})}, \quad ST_\mathrm{total} = 10 \lg \frac{E(20\,\mathrm{ms}, 1\,\mathrm{s})}{E(0, 10\,\mathrm{ms})}

The early ensemble level, for a musician hearing another across the platform, is the first 80 ms over the direct sound at 1 m from the source. In the engine, that reference is the free-field direct sound at 1 m, 100 \, E_{10}:

EEL = 10 \lg \frac{E(0, 80\,\mathrm{ms})}{100 \, E_{10}} = G_\mathrm{early} - 20 \;\mathrm{dB}

A reference measured on a platform also holds the floor's reflection, so a measured EEL can be up to 3 dB lower.

13. Speech: STI, STIPA, RaSTI, %ALcons, SII

The room's modulation transfer function

From the energy response, at modulation frequency F [Schroeder 1981; Houtgast and Steeneken 1985]:

m(F) = \frac{\left\lvert \sum_i h_i \, e^{-j 2\pi F (i + 1/2) \Delta t} \right\rvert}{\sum_i h_i}

Speech Transmission Index (IEC 60268-16)

In octave band k (125 Hz to 8 kHz), take the speech level L_{s,k}, the noise L_{n,k}, the reception threshold L_{rt,k} and auditory masking from the band below. Writing I(L) = 10^{(L - 93.98)/10}, the modulation left at each of the 14 modulation frequencies (0.63 to 12.5 Hz in third-octave steps) is:

m'_k(F) = m_k(F) \, \frac{I(L_{s,k})}{I(L_{s,k}) + I(L_{n,k}) + I(L_{rt,k}) + I_{am,k}}
SNR_\mathrm{eff} = \mathrm{clamp} \left( 10 \lg \frac{m'}{1 - m'}, \; -15, \; 15 \right), \quad TI = \frac{SNR_\mathrm{eff} + 15}{30}, \quad MTI_k = \frac{1}{14} \sum_{F} TI_k(F)
STI = \sum_{k=1}^{7} \alpha_k \, MTI_k - \sum_{k=1}^{6} \beta_k \sqrt{MTI_k \, MTI_{k+1}}

The result is rounded to 0.01. The masking intensity is I_{am,k} = \mathrm{amf} \cdot I(L_{k-1}), where L_{k-1} is the speech and noise of the band below added together, and amf steps with that level: 0 below 45 dB, then 10−4, 3.16×10−4, 3.16×10−4, 10−3, 3.16×10−3 for 45–55, 55–65, 65–75, 75–85 and 85–95 dB, and 0.1 above 95 dB. 14. Open-plan offices and workrooms

ISO 3382-3 (2012 and 2022)

Along a line of positions from one omnidirectional source, the speech is normal unisex speech (Table 1, 57.4 dB(A) at 1 m) taken to each position as L_{p,S} = L_{p,S,1m} + G - 20, A-weighted over 125 Hz to 8 kHz. The STI at each position is the full method, with that speech, the background noise and male weighting. Between 2 and 16 m from the source, a least-squares line L_{p,A,S} = A + B \log_2 r gives:

D_{2,S} = -B, \quad L_{p,A,S,4m} = A + 2B, \quad r_C = 2^{\left( L_{p,A,S,4m} - 45 + 2 D_{2,S} \right) / D_{2,S}}

The distraction and privacy distances, r_D and r_P, are where a straight line through STI against r (every position beyond 1 m) reaches 0.50 and 0.20. A distance outside the positions' range is extrapolated.

ISO 14257 workrooms

The sound distribution value in each band, 125 Hz to 4 kHz, is D_j = L_{p,j} - L_{W,j} = G_j - 31, and the free-field reference curve is D_\mathrm{ref}(r) = -20 \lg(r / 1\,\mathrm{m}) - 11. The frequency-normalised value (for A-weighted pink noise, P_j from Table 1) and the value for a machine of sound power L_{W,j} are:

D_A = 10 \lg \sum_j 10^{(D_j + P_j)/10} - 6.2, \qquad D_{L_W} = 10 \lg \sum_j 10^{(D_j + L_{W,j})/10} - 10 \lg \sum_j 10^{L_{W,j}/10}

The curve is described in three regions of distance from the source (clause 6.2): near, from 1 m to d_1; middle, from d_1 to d_2; and far, from d_2 on. The standard's typical values, d_1 = 5 m and d_2 = 16 m, are used unless others are set to suit the room, and the results then say which. A position on a boundary counts in both regions. In each region with at least three positions i = n \ldots m (z = m - n + 1 of them, in order of distance, x_i = \lg(r_i / 1\,\mathrm{m})), the decay per doubling of distance is Formula 5, the least-squares slope taken with 0.3 for \lg 2:

DL_2 = -0.3 \, \frac{z \sum D_i x_i - \sum D_i \sum x_i}{z \sum x_i^2 - \left(\sum x_i\right)^2}

The excess over the free-field curve, DL_{f,i} = D_i - D_\mathrm{ref}(r_i), is averaged over log distance by trapezoids (Formula 7):

DL_f(r_n, r_m) = \frac{\sum_{i=n+1}^{m} \left(DL_{f,i} + DL_{f,i-1}\right) \lg(r_i / r_{i-1})}{2 \lg(r_m / r_n)}

The regression line's height over the free-field curve at the region's conventional distance r, 4 m near, 10 m middle and 30 m far, is Formula 8:

DL'_{f,r} = \frac{1}{z}\sum D_i + 20 \lg r + \frac{DL_2}{\lg 2} \left( \frac{1}{z}\sum x_i - \lg r \right) + 11

Formula 8 divides by \lg 2 where Formula 5 multiplied by 0.3, so for a free field it gives a few hundredths of a decibel rather than zero; the standard's formulas are used as written. ISO 11690's typical values of DL_2 in the middle region are shown for comparison.

15. Reverberation formulas and the quick estimate

With the total surface area S, its mean absorption coefficient \bar{\alpha} = \sum S_i \alpha_i / S and the air's absorption area 4mV:

T = \frac{55.3 \, V}{c \, (A + 4 m V)}
A_\mathrm{Sabine} = S \bar{\alpha}, \quad A_\mathrm{Eyring} = -S \ln(1 - \bar{\alpha}), \quad A_\mathrm{Millington} = -\sum_i S_i \ln(1 - \alpha_i)

[Sabine 1922; Eyring 1930; Millington 1932; Sette 1933.] For Arau-Puchades and Fitzroy, the three pairs of faces x, y, z, with areas S_x, S_y, S_z, each get an Eyring time, T_x = 55.3V / \left[ c \left( -S \ln(1 - \bar{\alpha}_x) + 4mV \right) \right], and these are combined [Arau-Puchades 1988; Fitzroy 1959]:

T_\mathrm{Arau} = T_x^{S_x/S} \, T_y^{S_y/S} \, T_z^{S_z/S}, \qquad T_\mathrm{Fitzroy} = \frac{S_x}{S} T_x + \frac{S_y}{S} T_y + \frac{S_z}{S} T_z

A coefficient of 1 is taken as 0.999 inside a logarithm. Adding the air's absorption to every pair is this program's choice; neither author includes it.

Quick estimate

The quick estimate is a direct sound plus a diffuse field that decays exponentially at Eyring's time T. Relative to the free field at 10 m (as G is), for a source of directivity factor Q at distance r:

E_d = \frac{100 \, Q}{r^2}, \quad E_r = \frac{31200 \, T}{V} \quad \left( 31200 \approx \frac{100 \times 4 \times 4\pi}{0.161} \right)
G = 10 \lg (E_d + E_r), \quad r_c = \sqrt{\frac{Q V}{312 \, T}}
E(0, t) = E_d + E_r \left( 1 - e^{-13.82 \, t / T} \right), \quad E(t, \infty) = E_r \, e^{-13.82 \, t / T}

From these come D50, C50, C80, Gearly and Glate, and also:

T_s = \frac{E_r \, T / 13.82}{E_d + E_r}, \qquad m(F) = \frac{\left\lvert E_d + \frac{E_r}{1 + j \omega \tau} \right\rvert}{E_d + E_r}, \quad \omega = 2\pi F, \; \tau = \frac{T}{13.82}

Here 13.82 is \ln 10^6, a 60 dB decay. Several sources add their direct sounds, and share one reverberant field. The estimate cannot see the room's shape or where sound arrives from, so LF, LFC, LJ and ST are blank in it.

16. Listening: synthesis, head, IACC, Ambisonics

A pressure response from the energy

The ray tracer has no phase, so the pressure response is synthesised. In each band, Gaussian noise n_b is limited to the band (edges f/\sqrt{2} and f\sqrt{2}) and scaled, sample by sample, to the energy of the bin the sample is in [Kuttruff 1993; Vorländer 2020]:

p(t) = \sum_b n_b(t) \sqrt{\frac{E_b(\lfloor t / \Delta t \rfloor)}{\Delta t \, f_s}}

Each band then has the right energy envelope. This is a statistical model of the response, not its exact waveform. A dry signal is convolved with it by FFT. Signals are resampled by windowed sinc, with 16 zero crossings, a Hann window and the cutoff at the lower Nyquist frequency.

Head shadow and the ears' delay

Each ear is a small receiver, with each arrival weighted by the power gain of a rigid sphere, Brown and Duda's one-pole, one-zero model [Brown and Duda 1998]. With head radius a_h = 8.75 cm and \theta the angle between where the sound comes from and the ear's axis:

\lvert H(f, \theta) \rvert^2 = \frac{1 + \alpha(\theta)^2 x^2}{1 + x^2}, \quad x = \frac{\pi f a_h}{c}, \quad \alpha(\theta) = 1.05 + 0.95 \cos\left( \frac{180^\circ}{150^\circ} \theta \right)

The direct sound's arrival at each ear is moved by Woodworth's path round the sphere [Woodworth 1938]:

\Delta\ell(\theta) = \begin{cases} -a_h \cos\theta, \; \theta \le \pi/2 \\ a_h (\theta - \pi/2), \; \theta > \pi/2 \end{cases}

A measured head (a SOFA file, AES69 SimpleFreeFieldHRIR) can replace the sphere. Its power gain in each band is the mean of \lvert H(f) \rvert^2 over the band, from each measured direction's impulse response padded to at least 8192 samples.

IACC and BQI (ISO 3382-1 Annex A)

For the two ears' signals p_L and p_R, over a window from t_1 to t_2 after the direct sound:

IACF(\tau) = \frac{\int_{t_1}^{t_2} p_L(t) \, p_R(t + \tau) \, dt}{\sqrt{\int_{t_1}^{t_2} p_L^2 \, dt \int_{t_1}^{t_2} p_R^2 \, dt}}, \qquad IACC = \max_{\lvert \tau \rvert \le 1\,\mathrm{ms}} \lvert IACF(\tau) \rvert

The windows are:

  • Early, 0 to 80 ms.
  • Late, 80 ms to 1 s.
  • Whole, 0 to 1 s.

IACCE3 is the mean of the early values at 500 Hz, 1 kHz and 2 kHz, and IACCL3 the same for the late ones. The binaural quality index is BQI = 1 - IACC_{E3} [Hidaka, Beranek and Okano 1995]. This is the definition for two recorded ear signals.

IACC of a simulated head, from where the sound comes from

The ISO table and the maps (a head at each point, facing the source) and the Listen tab (the head as it faces there) take IACC from the directions the energy arrives from, not from the rendered ears: their noise is independent at each ear, so their own correlation would be low even at 63 Hz. Each arrival reaches the two ears as the same sound, scaled by each ear's shadow P_L, P_R (the power gain above) and delayed between them by Woodworth's path, \Delta = (\Delta\ell(\pi - \theta) - \Delta\ell(\theta)) / c. Different arrivals are incoherent with each other, as the traced energies are. For a sphere facing the source, only the lateral angle tells the ears apart. So the tracer gathers each window's energy E_k into 180 cells of lateral angle, one a degree, timed from the first arrival (the direct sound, or the earliest image path). Then:

IACF(\tau) = \frac{\sum_k E_k \sqrt{P_{L,k} P_{R,k}} \; \rho(\tau - \Delta_k)}{\sqrt{\sum_k E_k P_{L,k} \sum_k E_k P_{R,k}}}, \qquad \rho(\tau) = \frac{\sin 2\pi f_2 \tau - \sin 2\pi f_1 \tau}{2\pi (f_2 - f_1) \tau}

Here \rho is the autocorrelation of noise filling the octave band from f_1 = f/\sqrt{2} to f_2 = f\sqrt{2}. IACC is the largest \lvert IACF \rvert within ±1 ms, searched every 10 µs and at each cell's own delay. One arrival on its own gives 1 from any direction; a diffuse field gives nearly 1 at 63 Hz, falling to about 0.1 to 0.3 from 2 kHz up. The whole response (0 to 1 s, IACCA) uses the two windows' cells added, and IACCA3 is its mean over 500 Hz to 2 kHz.

Ambisonics, orders 1 to 3 (AmbiX: ACN order, SN3D)

A plane wave s from the unit direction (x, y, z) gives each channel s times its real spherical harmonic there [Nachbar et al. 2011]. The first order is:

W = s, \quad Y = s \, y, \quad Z = s \, z, \quad X = s \, x

The second order (ACN 4 to 8) is:

\sqrt{3}\,xy, \quad \sqrt{3}\,yz, \quad \tfrac{1}{2}(3z^2 - 1), \quad \sqrt{3}\,xz, \quad \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(x^2 - y^2)

The third order (ACN 9 to 15) is:

\sqrt{\tfrac{5}{8}}\,y(3x^2 - y^2), \quad \sqrt{15}\,xyz, \quad \sqrt{\tfrac{3}{8}}\,y(5z^2 - 1), \quad \tfrac{1}{2} z(5z^2 - 3), \quad \sqrt{\tfrac{3}{8}}\,x(5z^2 - 1), \quad \tfrac{\sqrt{15}}{2} z(x^2 - y^2), \quad \sqrt{\tfrac{5}{8}}\,x(x^2 - 3y^2)

With SN3D, a channel of order n holds 1/(2n + 1) of W's energy in a diffuse field.

The tracer keeps each bin's energy-weighted direction of arrival, \mathbf{v} = \sum E \, \hat{u}_\mathrm{from} / \sum E. As in DirAC [Pulkki 2007], its length \rho = \lvert \mathbf{v} \rvert splits the bin's amplitude A:

  • A \sqrt{\rho} is a plane wave from \hat{v}.
  • A \sqrt{1 - \rho} is a diffuse field: independent noise in every channel, scaled by 1/\sqrt{2n + 1} in a channel of order n.

Each bin has one mean direction. So higher orders sharpen the image of each arrival, but two arrivals in the same millisecond from different ways become one plane wave between them plus a diffuse part.

17. Measured impulse responses

A mono recording is analysed from its largest sample, taken as the direct sound, for at most the response length. Each octave band is cut out of the spectrum by a zero-phase filter with fourth-order Butterworth edges:

\lvert H(f) \rvert = \sqrt{\frac{(f/f_l)^{8}}{1 + (f/f_l)^{8}}} \, \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + (f/f_h)^{8}}}

The squared signal is gathered into 1 ms bins. Then:

  • The noise floor, the mean energy of the last tenth of the recording, is subtracted.
  • The response is cut where its short-time average (11 ms, or four periods of the band if longer) first comes within 3 dB of the floor. This is a simple stand-in for Lundeby's iterative method [Lundeby et al. 1995].
  • A decay time is given only if the band's dynamic range exceeds its fit range by 10 dB: 20 dB for EDT, 35 dB for T20 and 45 dB for T30.

The parameters then follow sections 9 and 10. Third-octave analysis uses the exact centres f_n = 1000 \cdot 10^{n/10} Hz.

18. Third-octave bands and maps

The engine's third-octave simulation (50 Hz to 8 kHz) runs three passes of eight bands. In each pass, the absorption and scattering are interpolated to the band centres, linearly in \log f and held at the ends. The source pattern is that of the octave band the centre is in, and the air absorption is taken at the centre itself. The Third octaves tab shows them at the main receiver; everything else is in octave bands.

A map is one ray set traced against every receiver of a grid. A receiver index tests a segment only against the receivers in the grid cells it crosses. The grid can be:

  • A plan at a height.
  • A vertical section.
  • Several heights.
  • Points at a height above a chosen surface.

Every point is kept clear of the surfaces by the receiver radius. Each point gets the parameters above, in all eight bands. Contours are drawn by marching squares over the grid.

19. Limits of the method

  • Wave effects. Geometric acoustics is not reliable below about the Schroeder frequency, f_S \approx 2000 \sqrt{T/V}, where room modes dominate [Kuttruff 2016].
  • Interference. Energy is traced, so there is no interference between paths, except within a coherent line array's own pattern.
  • Diffraction. It is modelled in three places: at screens' free edges, round the room's own corners for the direct sound and for image paths of up to two reflections (both section 8), and as scattering at surface edges (7.3). Higher-order reflections that a corner hides, and the ray-traced sound, do not bend round it.
  • Absorption. Unless the angle setting is on, a surface's absorption is the same at every angle of incidence. With it, a surface is a plain locally reacting one; the seat dip over audience is not modelled either way.
  • Synthesised responses. The sound you listen to has the right energy envelope in each band, but not the true waveform.
